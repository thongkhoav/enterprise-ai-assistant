# Customer Success Playbook

This playbook helps customer success managers prepare for enterprise onboarding, quarterly business
reviews, renewal risk discussions, and escalation follow-up. It focuses on operational signals that
an AI assistant can retrieve and summarize for customer-facing teams.

## Account Health

Account health combines usage, support load, product adoption, billing status, stakeholder
engagement, and incident history. A healthy enterprise account uses at least three core workflows,
has active administrators, completes onboarding milestones, and has no unresolved P0/P1 issues.

Accounts at risk usually show declining weekly active users, repeated import failures, unpaid
invoices, low executive engagement, missed onboarding milestones, or multiple support escalations in
the last 30 days.

## Onboarding Milestones

Standard onboarding includes kickoff, workspace provisioning, administrator training, data import
preflight, production import, SSO configuration, entitlement review, pilot launch, success criteria
review, and full rollout. Strategic customers also receive a technical architecture review and a
joint launch readiness checkpoint.

If a customer misses two onboarding milestones, the CSM should create a risk note, update the
account owner, and schedule a recovery planning call. If the missed milestone is related to data
import, data operations should review the preflight report before the next call.

## Quarterly Business Review

QBR preparation should include usage trend, feature adoption, support ticket volume, SLA performance,
open risks, incident summary, roadmap asks, renewal date, expansion opportunities, and executive
stakeholder notes. The QBR deck should not include raw support transcripts or internal incident
discussion.

The CSM should ask the assistant for a policy-backed summary rather than a generic account summary.
Useful questions include: "Which unresolved risks could affect renewal?", "What import issues have
blocked onboarding?", and "Which policy applies to expedited refund handling?"

## Renewal Risk

Renewal risk is high when there is an unresolved P0, three or more P1 tickets in a quarter, repeated
SLA misses, executive sponsor departure, non-payment, or adoption below 40 percent of licensed
users. Renewal risk is medium when there are unresolved configuration issues, delayed onboarding,
or feature gaps with known workarounds.

For high-risk renewals, the CSM should create a recovery plan with owner, target outcome, customer
commitment, Acme commitment, timeline, and executive sponsor. Do not mark the account green until
the customer confirms the recovery plan is working.

## Escalation Follow-Up

After an escalation, the CSM should confirm whether the customer understands the resolution, whether
any compensation or refund request exists, whether internal corrective actions are assigned, and
whether the account health score should change.

If an escalation involved security, billing, or data loss, the CSM must coordinate with the
responsible operations team before making commitments. CSMs should not promise credits or security
attestations without approval.

## Executive Updates

Executive updates should be short and specific: customer impact, business risk, current owner, next
milestone, decision needed, and date of next update. Avoid including long technical details unless
the executive audience requested them.

When a customer asks for a written executive update, the CSM should use customer-safe language and
remove internal system names, private employee notes, and vendor IDs.
